## Before You Give Up: Try This First

Most Wahl Peanut "not cutting" complaints are solved by one thing: **cleaning and oiling the blade.** It takes 2 minutes and fixes the problem 80% of the time.

Here's the sequence to work through, starting with the easiest fix:

---

## Fix #1: Clean and Oil the Blade (Try This First)

Hair, skin debris, and dried oil build up between the blade teeth and clog the cutting action. The clipper sounds like it's running fine, but the blade can't move freely.

**How to do it:**
1. Power off the clipper
2. Remove the blade (slide forward and off)
3. Brush out debris with the cleaning brush
4. Run the brush between the blade teeth in both directions
5. Reattach the blade
6. Add **3–4 drops of Wahl clipper oil** along the top edge of the blade teeth
7. Power on and run for 20 seconds to distribute the oil
8. Test on hair

If this doesn't fix it, move to Fix #2.

---

## Fix #2: Check Blade Alignment

A slightly misaligned blade will cut unevenly or not cut at all on one side.

**How to check:**
1. Look at the blade head-on
2. The top (moving) blade should be slightly behind the bottom (stationary) blade — about 1–2mm set back
3. The blade should be centered — not shifted left or right

**If it's misaligned:**
1. Loosen (don't remove) the two blade screws
2. Reposition the blade so it's centered and set back correctly
3. Tighten the screws back up
4. Test

---

## Fix #3: Check the Taper Lever Position

This is a common beginner mistake: the taper lever is set too closed, which limits how much the blade teeth pass over each other.

Look at the lever on the left side of the clipper:
- **Lever down = more open** (longer cut, more guard clearance)
- **Lever up = more closed** (shorter cut, tighter blade)

If the lever is fully closed during a pass, the blade can feel like it's not cutting. Try moving the lever to the middle or open position.

---

## Fix #4: Replace the Blade

If cleaning, oiling, and alignment don't fix it — the blade is likely dull or damaged.

**How to tell if you need a new blade:**
- Visible nicks or chips in the teeth (run your thumbnail lightly across — you'll feel irregularities)
- The blade has been in use 12+ months with regular use
- The blade has been dropped on a hard floor (this can misalign or chip the teeth)

A replacement blade is ~$15 and takes 2 minutes to install. See our [blade replacement guide](/how-to/change-wahl-peanut-blade) for the step-by-step.

---

## Fix #5: Check for Motor Issues

If the blade sounds labored, slow, or the clipper feels underpowered — and you've ruled out blade issues — the motor may be the culprit.

**Signs of motor problems:**
- The clipper vibrates excessively or unevenly
- The blade barely moves even with a new, oiled replacement
- The trimmer cuts fine on thin hair but stalls on thicker sections

For a corded Peanut, try a different outlet. Power fluctuations can affect motor performance.

For a cordless Peanut: check battery charge. Low battery causes noticeably reduced cutting power.

> **If the motor is the issue:** Wahl offers repair service, but at the Peanut's price point, a replacement unit is often more economical. The corded model is ~$30.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**My Wahl Peanut pulls hair even after oiling — what next?**
The blade is likely dull. Even a clean, oiled dull blade will pull. Replace the blade.

**How often should I oil the blade?**
Every use, or at minimum every few uses. Before you clip, add 2–3 drops and run for 10 seconds.

**Can I use regular cooking or machine oil?**
No. Use clipper-specific oil. Cooking oil goes rancid and clogs the blade; machine oil is too thick.
