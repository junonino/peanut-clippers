## Is Some Heat Normal?

Yes — all electric clippers generate heat during operation. The Peanut's motor drives the blade at high speed, which creates friction. Some warmth after 10–15 minutes of use is completely normal.

**What's not normal:**
- The blade becoming hot enough to be uncomfortable to touch after just 2–3 minutes
- Heat that feels burning against skin
- The heat increasing rapidly regardless of use duration

If your Peanut is uncomfortably hot quickly, something is causing excess friction. Let's find it.

---

## Cause #1: Dry Blade (Most Common)

Without oil, metal rubs against metal at thousands of strokes per minute. Friction = heat. This is the cause in the majority of overheating complaints.

**Fix:**
1. Power off and let cool for 2 minutes
2. Apply **3–4 drops of Wahl blade oil** across the top of the blade teeth
3. Power on and run for 20 seconds to distribute
4. Resume cutting

From now on: **oil the blade before every session.** It takes 5 seconds and completely prevents friction-related overheating.

---

## Cause #2: Hair Buildup Between the Blades

Packed-in hair and debris reduce the gap between the blades, increasing metal-on-metal contact and friction. This is especially common if you don't clean between sessions.

**Fix:**
1. Power off
2. Remove the blade
3. Brush out all debris — top, bottom, and in the channel between them
4. Blow out any remaining fine particles
5. Oil and reattach

> Do this after **every session** to prevent buildup. A 30-second clean after each use prevents 90% of blade performance issues.

---

## Cause #3: Blade Screws Too Tight

Over-tightened blade screws press the blade too firmly against the clipper body, restricting airflow and increasing friction.

**Fix:**
1. Loosen the blade screws slightly (just a quarter-turn)
2. The blade should sit flush but not feel clamped

---

## Cause #4: Extended Continuous Use

The Peanut is designed for intermittent use during a session — not continuous operation for 30+ minutes without pause. Motors heat up with sustained running.

**Fix:**
- During a long session, give the clipper a 2–3 minute rest every 20 minutes
- Alternatively, keep two Peanuts and rotate between them (common in busy barber shops)
- Use blade coolant spray for fast cool-downs between clients

---

## Cause #5: Dull or Damaged Blade

A dull blade works harder to cut, which means more friction and more heat. If you've oiled regularly and it's still running hot, blade deterioration may be the cause.

**Check:** Run your thumbnail lightly across the blade teeth. A sharp blade should feel smooth and even. Nicks or irregular edges indicate a blade that needs replacing.

---

## At What Temperature Should You Stop?

A simple test: touch the blade to the inside of your wrist. If it's uncomfortable to hold there for 3 seconds, it's too hot to use on a client's skin. Power off and let it cool.

As a reference, blade temperatures above ~130°F (54°C) can cause discomfort on sensitive skin.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Should I use blade coolant spray on the Wahl Peanut?**
Yes — products like Andis Cool Care or Wahl's own spray are safe and effective. They cool the blade quickly and also lubricate and help prevent rust.

**My Peanut gets hot even after oiling — is it broken?**
If oil doesn't help, check blade alignment (over-tightened screws) and clean for hair buildup. If it still runs abnormally hot, the blade may need replacement.

**Can I put the hot blade in cold water to cool it faster?**
No. Thermal shock can warp or crack the blade. Let it air cool or use a proper blade coolant spray.
