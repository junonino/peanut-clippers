## The Bottom Line Upfront

This isn't really a performance comparison — it's a **use-case comparison.** The blade, motor, and cut quality are nearly identical. The question is whether you need a cord or not.

---

## When Corded Wins

**You're a shop barber at a fixed station.** Your outlet is right there, your cord is managed with a clip, and you never think about it. That $25 difference adds up to a nicer lunch.

**You cut hair for hours at a stretch.** The corded Peanut never loses power. No battery fade, no mid-session charge worry, no gradual slowdown. It's consistent from opening to close.

**Budget is a priority.** At ~$30, the corded Peanut is one of the best-value professional trimmers on the market. The cordless is excellent, but that $25 premium is real money.

---

## When Cordless Wins

**You're mobile.** If you travel to clients, the cord is a genuine problem. Setup time, tangling, outlet hunting — the cordless model removes all of that friction.

**You cut at home.** Doing your own fade in the bathroom? A cord stringing across your sink or draped over your shoulder is annoying. The cordless just feels cleaner.

**You want backup flexibility.** The Peanut Li can run plugged in if the battery dies. You essentially get both options in one trimmer.

**You travel with it.** Throwing a cordless trimmer in a bag for a trip is simple. A corded trimmer means packing that extra cable and occupying an outlet.

---

## Battery Life: Is 60 Minutes Really Enough?

For **at-home users** (1–2 cuts per week): yes, easily. You'll charge it once every week or two.

For **professionals doing multiple clients per day:** it depends. If you're doing 5–6 clients and using the Peanut for 10–15 minutes per client, you'll need to charge it daily. That's completely manageable.

**Pro tip:** Keep it on the charging stand between sessions. Always start at 100%.

---

## The Power Fade Question

One honest note about cordless performance: **lithium ion batteries deliver slightly less torque near empty.** Wahl's motor manages this well, but if you're cutting very coarse or dense hair and notice the blade slowing in the final 10 minutes of battery — that's why.

The corded model has zero consistency issues.
