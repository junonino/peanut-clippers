## When Do You Need a New Blade?

Before we get into the how-to, let's make sure you actually need a new blade — not just a cleaning and oil.

**Signs your Wahl Peanut blade needs replacing:**
- Hair is pulling instead of cutting cleanly
- The clipper snags on hair repeatedly
- Visible nicks or chips on the blade teeth
- Blade no longer adjusts smoothly with the taper lever
- It's been 12+ months of regular use without replacement

If the blade is just dirty or dry, try **cleaning and oiling it first** before spending $15 on a replacement. A dirty blade often mimics a dull one.

---

## Step 1: Turn Off and Unplug the Clipper

Safety first. Fully power off the clipper and unplug it (or for the cordless model, ensure it's powered off). Never change a blade with the unit running.

---

## Step 2: Locate the Blade Assembly

The blade assembly is the metal component at the front tip of the Peanut. It's held in place by two screws on either side of the blade.

You'll see:
- The **top blade** (the moving part that does the cutting)
- The **bottom blade** (stationary, sits against the clipper body)
- Two **blade screws** (Phillips head)

---

## Step 3: Remove the Blade

Wahl Peanut blades are designed to slide off without tools on newer models. Try this first:

1. Hold the clipper body firmly in one hand
2. With your thumb and forefinger, grip the blade assembly
3. Slide the blade **forward** (toward the cutting edge) and then **lift up**
4. The blade should pop off cleanly

> **If it won't budge:** Use a small Phillips screwdriver to loosen (don't fully remove) the two blade screws. Once loosened slightly, the blade will slide free.

---

## Step 4: Clean the Blade Area

Before attaching the new blade:

1. Brush out any hair stuck in the blade seat with the cleaning brush
2. Wipe the blade seat with a clean, dry cloth
3. Do NOT use water — moisture causes rust

---

## Step 5: Attach the New Blade

1. Line up the new blade with the blade seat — there are two small posts that align with holes in the blade
2. Slide the blade **backward** (toward the body) until it clicks or sits flush
3. If you loosened the screws, re-tighten them now — firm but not over-tightened

The blade should sit flat, with no gap between the blade and the clipper body.

---

## Step 6: Oil the New Blade

Never run a new blade dry. Before the first use:

1. Add **2–3 drops of clipper oil** along the top of the blade teeth
2. Run the clipper for 15–20 seconds to distribute the oil
3. Wipe away any excess with a cloth

> **Pro tip:** Oil your blade at the start of every session as a habit. It takes 5 seconds and doubles blade life.

---

## Step 7: Test the Blade

Run the clipper and check:
- ✓ The blade runs smoothly without stuttering
- ✓ The taper lever moves the blade without resistance
- ✓ No unusual noise or vibration

If something feels off, power off and check the blade alignment — it may not be fully seated.

## Blade Replacement Frequency

| Usage level | Replacement interval |
|---|---|
| Occasional (home, 1x/week) | Every 12–18 months |
| Regular (barber, 5–10 clients/day) | Every 3–6 months |
| Heavy (barber, 10+ clients/day) | Every 4–8 weeks |

Daily oiling dramatically extends blade life at any frequency.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I put any blade on a Wahl Peanut?**
No. Wahl Peanut blades are model-specific. Only use blades labeled for the Wahl Peanut (not generic Wahl blades — the form factor is different).

**Do I need to break in a new blade?**
Not exactly, but running it oiled for a minute before the first cut helps seat everything properly.

**My blade keeps falling off — what's wrong?**
The blade screws may be stripped or the blade seat is damaged. Check both screws are seated properly. If the screws are stripped, replacement screws or a new blade assembly are needed.
